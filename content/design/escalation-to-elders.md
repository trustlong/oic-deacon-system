# Escalation to Elders

Elders are for spiritual oversight, soul care, and policy authority. Deacons protect that by resolving everything tangible themselves. This note defines the exact conditions that send a request to an elder, how to handle mixed requests, and how to make the hand-off warm.

---

## The Tangible-vs-Spiritual Test

Every request the duty deacon receives has a **core need**. Ask: *Is the core need something a person can meet with time, money, skill, or a physical resource — or does it require spiritual authority, pastoral wisdom, or soul care?*

| If the need is... | It goes to... |
|---|---|
| Food, money, transportation, housing, a physical resource | Deacons |
| Prayer, encouragement, a pastoral check-in | PEP sub-lane (Communication / deacons) |
| Grief counseling, marriage difficulty, spiritual crisis | Elder |
| Church discipline, restoration | Elder |
| Doctrinal question or theological confusion | Elder |
| A policy threshold (see below) | Elder concurrence required |
| Mixed (tangible + spiritual) | Split — deacons keep tangible, elder gets spiritual |

See [[handbook-tangible-vs-spiritual]] for the fuller framework from the church handbook.

---

## Exact Escalation Triggers

Escalate to an elder **only** when one or more of these conditions is true:

1. **Spiritual in nature.** The core need is soul care, grief counseling, spiritual direction, faith-and-life counsel, or prayer that is pastoral rather than intercessory.
2. **Requires spiritual authority.** Church discipline, restoration, membership status, elder-led accountability.
3. **Policy threshold — Benevolence.** The requested disbursement would materially reduce the benevolence fund. See [[benevolence-roles]] for the current threshold definition.
4. **Policy threshold — Building spend.** A building-related expenditure that exceeds $12,500 requires elder concurrence before the deacons can commit.

If none of these four conditions applies, the request stays with the deacons regardless of how sensitive or difficult the situation feels.

---

## The Split-Mixed-Request Pattern

Most requests that seem to involve elders are actually mixed: there is a tangible component and a spiritual component. The pattern is always the same — **deacons keep the tangible parts, only the spiritual part goes to an elder.**

### Example

A widow calls in. She has not paid rent in two months and is grieving her husband's death. She is struggling spiritually and asking whether God has abandoned her.

| Component | Who owns it |
|---|---|
| Two months of back rent (financial assistance) | Finance / Benevolence owner |
| Groceries while she gets back on her feet | Operations / Kitchen owner |
| Grief counseling, spiritual crisis ("has God abandoned me?") | Elder |

The duty deacon routes the financial and grocery need through the normal [[routing-and-functions|resource priority pass]]. Then makes the warm hand-off to an elder for the spiritual piece. The deacons do not wait for the elder to touch the tangible need — those are worked in parallel.

### Why This Matters

Sending the entire request to an elder buries them in logistics. The elder's gift is spiritual care, not coordinating grocery delivery. Split the request, and the elder gets a clean, focused handoff: "We are handling the financial and food needs. Here is the spiritual situation."

## The Warm Hand-Off to an Elder

When a spiritual escalation is needed, the duty deacon (or owner) makes the hand-off **directly and personally** — not by telling the member "you should go talk to an elder."

### How to do it

1. Identify a specific elder by name. (Not "an elder" — *a specific elder*.)
2. Call or meet with that elder before the member calls them. Provide a one-paragraph summary (see template below).
3. Tell the member: "I've spoken with [Elder Name], and they're expecting your call / they'll be reaching out to you." Give the member the elder's contact information.
4. Log the hand-off in the tracker: elder's name, date, summary shared. Status → **Awaiting Elder** (or **Redirected** if the deacon role is fully complete).

### One-Paragraph Summary Template

> "[Member name] reached out on [date] with a need for [brief tangible description — already being handled by the diaconate]. During intake, it became clear there is also a spiritual dimension: [one or two sentences describing the spiritual need in the member's own words or close paraphrase]. The member is open to pastoral conversation. Contact: [phone/email]. I've let them know you'll be in touch."

This summary should take the elder from cold to context in under 30 seconds. Keep it brief. Include what the deacons are already handling so the elder doesn't re-cover that ground.

---

## What Not To Do

- **Never** tell a member to "go find an elder." This is a bounce, not a hand-off.
- **Never** send a group email to the elder board — contact one named elder.
- **Never** delay working the tangible need while waiting for elder involvement. Split the work and run in parallel.
- **Never** escalate to an elder because a situation is emotionally difficult or complicated. Difficult is not the same as spiritual. Deacons are equipped for hard tangible situations.
