> **Goal:** clear, self-explanatory urgency tiers and the response timeline a responder owes for each — so deacons know what "fast enough" means.

## To design here

- The urgency tiers (e.g. Emergency / Urgent / Routine) with concrete definitions and examples.
- For each tier: time-to-acknowledge, time-to-assign, time-to-first-action, and check-in cadence.
- How urgency is set at intake and who can change it.
- Alignment with existing policy: the Benevolence Policy already says urgent needs are handled *"within a few days or sooner for emergencies,"* with follow-up *"within a few weeks."* (see [[benevolence-request-workflow]]).

## Draft tiers

| Tier | Definition | Acknowledge | First action | Follow-up |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Emergency | Safety / shutoff / eviction today | same day | same day | within days |
| Urgent | Real risk within ~1 week | within 1 day | within a few days | ~1 week |
| Routine | No imminent deadline | within 2–3 days | as scheduled | as needed |
