## Biblical Basis

The office of deacon is grounded in **Acts 6:1-7**, where seven men were set apart to coordinate a solution to a tangible problem so that the apostles would not be distracted from prayer and the ministry of the word. Four New Testament passages bear on the office: Acts 6:1-7, Philippians 1:1, Romans 16:1, and 1 Timothy 3:8-12.

The word *deacon* derives from the Greek *diakonos*, meaning servant. While the term is generic, it also designates a formal church office. As the handbook puts it:

> "Deacons are model servants appointed to a local church office. They are deployed to assist the pastors or elders by protecting church unity, organizing practical service, and meeting tangible needs."

Deacons are described as "a cavalry of servants, deputized to execute the elders' vision by coordinating various ministries. When deacons flourish, the whole congregation wins."

## The Role Is Not About Honor

The role of deacon is not to be sought for personal honor. It is a call to a greater commitment to service. Paul wrote that faithful deacons "obtain for themselves a good standing and great boldness in the faith which is in Christ Jesus" (1 Tim. 3:13). The selfless deacon shares kinship with Christ, who walked the earth as "One who serves" (Luke 22:27).

## Three Core Diaconal Functions

1. **Spotting and Meeting Tangible Needs** — Deacons are skilled at identifying practical needs and taking initiative to meet them efficiently, freeing elders to focus on their primary responsibilities.

2. **Protecting and Promoting Church Unity** — Deacons serve as "shock absorbers" in congregational life, building the unity the Bible commands. The installation of the seven in Acts 6 was ultimately to salvage the unity of the Jerusalem church.

3. **Serving and Supporting the Ministry of the Elders** — By enabling elders to stay focused on teaching and prayer, deacons guard and advance the ministry of the word. They are like a congregation's offensive linemen protecting the quarterback. Deacons are never presented as chaperones who impose a check on elder decisions; in a healthy church, godly deacons execute the vision of godly elders.

## What Deacons Are Not

At OIC, deacons are not "elders-in-training," nor a counterweight to the elders to check and balance pastoral decisions.
