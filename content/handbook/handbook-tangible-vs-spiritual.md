## The Dividing Line

The office of deacon and the office of elder are distinct, with different aims that often require different gifts (OIC Bylaws, Sections 2 and 3).

- **Deacons** handle **tangible and temporal needs** — the outward, physical, and practical dimensions of church life: facilities, finances, administration, communications, mercy, and coordination.
- **Elders** hold **spiritual authority and soul care** — the ministry of the word, prayer, teaching, governance, and the oversight of doctrine and membership.

The handbook is explicit:

> "Deacons shall not exercise spiritual authority..."

All amendments to the Deacons Handbook must remain within the four authorized areas of responsibility (Operations, Finances, Administration, Communications) and **shall not assign spiritual authority to Deacons** (Bylaws Article 2, Section 3b; Handbook Appendix 2).

## Redirecting Non-Tangible Requests

When a deacon encounters a need that is spiritual or non-tangible in nature — questions of doctrine, pastoral counsel, church discipline, soul care — the appropriate response is to redirect the person to the elders or pastoral staff.

The goal is that people are never left without a path to the right help. The [[handbook-communications|Communications Department]] bears responsibility for making "it easy for people to get the help and support they need from the elders and pastoral staff or church body as a whole."

## Why This Distinction Matters

The distinction is not about hierarchy but about function and calling. By staying within their lane, deacons protect the elders' ability to devote themselves to prayer and the ministry of the word — exactly the pattern established in Acts 6. See [[handbook-what-is-a-deacon|What Is a Deacon?]] for the biblical foundation.
