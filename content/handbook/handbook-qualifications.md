## Biblical Basis

OIC Bylaw 3.3a.3 states that "a deacon shall be qualified for the office as specified in the Bible (1 Timothy 3:8–12)." This is about who a deacon must **be**, not what they are able to **do**. Paul's list centers on being "dignified" (worthy of respect) — three things a deacon must not be, and three things a deacon must be.

## Three Things a Deacon Must Not Be

All three relate to self-control.

1. **Not Double-Tongued** — Not saying one thing to one group and something else to another. A deacon guards their tongue against disclosing what a person doesn't need to know, and guards their ears against gossip or slander about leaders or members.
2. **Not Addicted to Much Wine** — Self-controlled in appetites, not just speech; a qualified deacon does not indulge cravings or abuse substances that would hinder their work or witness.
3. **Not Greedy for Dishonest Gain** — Self-controlled with money. Diaconal work sometimes involves handling church funds, so a deacon should not be known for deception, cutting moral corners, or an unhealthy focus on money.

## Three Things a Deacon Must Be

4. **Holds the Mystery of the Faith with a Clear Conscience** — "Mystery" here means the content of the gospel and Christian doctrine. Teaching and governing is primarily the elders' role, but a deacon is not exempt from knowing their Bible, since they will often have opportunities to speak biblical truth. This requires a deacon to **know** the faith (grow in clear understanding), **hold** the faith (grasp it with the heart, not just the mind), and **live** the faith (moral integrity through ongoing confession and repentance — 1 John 1:9).
5. **Tested and Proven** — Scripture requires a season of testing before service, without specifying its length. OIC has adopted **one year of church membership** (Bylaw 3.3a.4) as that testing period, during which a candidate's character, convictions, practices, strengths, and weaknesses are observed. See [[bylaws-deacons|Bylaws: The Office of Deacon]] for the membership prerequisite and full nomination process.
6. **Faithful Family Life** — A deacon's godliness begins with their closest relationships: faithfulness to a spouse if married, and raising children (if any) in gentle firmness and the joyful love of God.

## How This Is Used

These six qualities describe ordinary Christian character, exemplified consistently rather than perfectly. The **Deacon Nomination Committee** evaluates candidates against 1 Timothy 3:8–13 as part of the nomination process — see [[bylaws-deacons|Bylaws: The Office of Deacon]] for that process end to end, and [[bylaws-elections|Annual Nominations & Elections]] for the yearly calendar.
