## OIC's Form of Government

OIC (One In Christ) Church practices an **elder-led, congregational** polity, as established in Article V of the OIC Constitution. The handbook captures this in two summary statements:

> *"Elder-led, Deacon-served, Congregation-ruled."*

> *"Elders lead ministry. Deacons facilitate ministry. The congregation does ministry."*

## The Three Authorities

| Role | Function |
|---|---|
| **Elders** | Lead and govern; hold spiritual authority; devoted to prayer and the ministry of the word |
| **Deacons** | Facilitate; coordinate practical service; execute the elders' vision |
| **Congregation** | The final earthly authority under Christ; decides doctrine, membership, leadership, and finances |

## Congregational Authority

The congregation is the final earthly authority under Christ's divine authority (Acts 6:1-6; Matthew 18:17; 2 Corinthians 2:6). On matters that are both serious and clearly addressed in Scripture — doctrine, leadership selection, membership, and overall budget — the Bible's pattern is for leadership to give sound, prayerful counsel, but for the church to decide.

### Two Unwanted Extremes

The handbook warns against two failures in congregationalism:

- **Absent congregationalism** — The congregation has no say; leaders decide everything unilaterally, even on the most important issues.
- **Hyper-congregationalism** — Almost every decision, including trivial ones (e.g., light bulbs, wall color), requires a church-wide vote.

Both extremes are unhelpful and unbiblical.

### Decision-Making Quadrant

The handbook provides a four-category framework based on two axes: **seriousness** (trivial → crucial) and **biblical clarity** (unclear → clearly taught):

| Category | Description | Who Decides |
|---|---|---|
| Neither serious nor clear | Preferences, minor operational choices (e.g., internet provider, snacks, thermostat) | Ministry teams / department leaders |
| Clear but not serious | Obvious answers, low stakes (e.g., replace a broken window, buy toilet paper) | Responsible leader or deacon |
| Serious but not clear | Weighty matters without specific biblical instruction (e.g., baptism age) | Elders; may be brought to congregation depending on seriousness |
| Both serious and clear | Doctrine, leadership, membership, finances | Congregation (with leadership counsel) |

## Relationship Between the Offices

The office of elders and the office of deacons are distinct with different aims, often requiring different gifts (OIC Bylaws, Sections 2 and 3). Deacons are not elders-in-training, nor a counterweight to check and balance pastoral decisions. See [[handbook-what-is-a-deacon|What Is a Deacon?]] and [[handbook-tangible-vs-spiritual|Tangible vs. Spiritual Needs]] for how the offices relate in practice.
