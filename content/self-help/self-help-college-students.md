College life in a new city can be great — and also unexpectedly hard. This page gathers practical resources for students in the Lynchburg area. The deacons keep it current.

**Need personal help?** [[need-help|Ask a deacon.]] We mean it — whether it's a ride, a rough week, or not knowing where to start.

## Transportation

Not having a car in Lynchburg is manageable with some planning.

- **Greater Lynchburg Transit Company (GLTC)** — local bus routes and schedules: [gltconline.com](https://www.gltconline.com)
- **Uber / Lyft** — both operate in the Lynchburg area
- **Your campus shuttle** — check with your school's transportation office; some campuses run shuttles to grocery stores and other destinations
- **Church rides** — church members regularly offer rides to Sunday services and sometimes to appointments or errands; ask a deacon to connect you

**Liberty University students:** Liberty Transit runs frequent free bus routes (Routes 71/72/75 cover main campus, East Campus, and Liberty Mountain) roughly every 6-7 minutes on weekdays, plus an on-demand ride service daily 5:30am–1am. Call (434) 592-3640 or email TransitDispatch@liberty.edu.

**University of Lynchburg / Randolph College students:** check with your school's transportation or student life office directly — their shuttle details are less consistently published online than Liberty's.

## Budgeting Basics

Getting intentional about money early makes a real difference.

- **CFPB (Consumer Financial Protection Bureau) — Money as You Grow:** practical budgeting guides for young adults: [consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/money-as-you-grow](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/money-as-you-grow/)
- **MyMoney.gov** — free government financial literacy resource: [mymoney.gov](https://www.mymoney.gov)
- **YNAB (You Need a Budget)** — popular budgeting app; offers a free trial and a student discount: [youneedabudget.com](https://www.youneedabudget.com)

## Food Assistance

If you are running short on food, please do not go hungry.

- **Your campus food pantry** — many colleges have a free, confidential food pantry for students; check with Student Services or your Dean of Students office
- **SNAP (food stamps)** — college students may be eligible in certain circumstances (e.g., working enough hours or meeting other criteria): [fns.usda.gov/snap/students](https://www.fns.usda.gov/snap/students)
- **Feeding America food bank locator:** [feedingamerica.org/find-your-local-foodbank](https://www.feedingamerica.org/find-your-local-foodbank)
- **211.org** — dial or text **211** to find local food pantries and emergency food near you: [211.org](https://www.211.org)

If you are unsure whether you qualify for anything here, [[need-help|ask a deacon]] — we can help you figure it out.

**Lynchburg-area food pantries** *(open to anyone, not just members of a particular church — call ahead to confirm hours)*:
- **Blue Ridge Area Food Bank** — 501 12th St; Mon–Fri 8am–4:30pm (closed for lunch)
- **Lynchburg Daily Bread** — free hot carry-out lunch daily, 10:15am–12:20pm
- **The Fillin' Station (Timberlake Church)** — Mon 5–6:30pm, Fri 11am–12:30pm

## Move-In and Move-Out Help

The beginning and end of the school year involve a lot of heavy lifting — sometimes literally.

- If you need help moving in or out, **ask a deacon** well in advance; church members have been glad to pitch in with trucks, muscle, and boxes
- **FreeCycle** — a community network for free items, including boxes and packing materials: [freecycle.org](https://www.freecycle.org)

## Campus Resources

Your school has more support than most students realize. A few worth knowing about:

- **Counseling center** — most campuses offer free or low-cost confidential counseling; look it up before you need it
- **Financial aid office** — if your situation changes mid-year (job loss, family hardship), talk to financial aid; emergency grants sometimes exist
- **Dean of Students office** — a good first stop for almost any non-academic problem; they know what resources are available
