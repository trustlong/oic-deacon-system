*Article 1, Section 4*

## Purpose

Church discipline is pursued to honor the name of Christ, protect the flock, educate and edify the body, and ensure purity. It is always conducted in hope that an erring member will repent and be restored to proper fellowship with the Lord and the church. It is the responsibility of the [[bylaws-elders|Elders]] to follow through on discipline; any recommendation brought before the church is made by the Elders.

Discipline applies to any [[bylaws-membership|member]] known to be committing acts of unchristian conduct or who consistently upsets the unity of the church.

## Process

A reasonable effort must first be made to secure a **private resolution** before any public action.

| Step | Action | Outcome if Unresolved |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Private, one-on-one admonition by a congregation member with personal knowledge of the conduct | Proceed to Step B |
| **B** | Second admonition in the presence of one or two other [[bylaws-elders\|Elders]] | Proceed to Step C |
| **C** | Public correction before the gathered church at a [[bylaws-meetings\|Members' Meeting]] | If this also fails, see sanctions below |

### Possible Sanctions (after Step C fails)

1. Public admonition
2. Removal from leadership position
3. Expulsion from fellowship and termination of membership — requires **85% majority** of voting members present

## Restoration

- If at any point the accused repents and makes appropriate restitution, restoration is considered accomplished and the process ends.
- If a previously dismissed member later repents, publicly confesses, and makes appropriate restitution, the disciplinary process is considered ended and the individual may again request [[bylaws-membership|membership]].
