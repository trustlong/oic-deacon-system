# Request Workflow

All benevolence requests follow this 7-step process. The church strives to respond promptly — often within a few days, sooner for genuine emergencies.

1. **Initiation** — The member contacts the Benevolence Team, the designated Deacon of Benevolence, or a pastor/elder to obtain the [[benevolence-request-form|Benevolence Request Form]]. The form is available from the church office or via a private online link. Verbal requests are accepted in urgent situations; the form is completed as soon as possible afterward.

2. **Initial Screening** — The team verifies that the applicant is an active covenant member and that the request falls within the scope of eligible needs. Requests that are clearly outside policy are declined promptly with a kind explanation, rather than raising false hopes.

3. **Information Gathering & Pastoral Consultation** — A team member reaches out for a personal conversation (in person or by phone) to clarify details, express care, and understand the full situation. Gentle questions explore how the need arose, what steps have already been taken, and whether non-financial resources (e.g., a professional in the congregation, financial counseling) might address the root problem.

4. **Evaluation** — The team prayerfully weighs the **severity and urgency** of the need, the **essential nature** of the expense, the member's **personal responsibility** in the situation, and the **current fund balance**. Each case is evaluated on its own merits. The team may consider whether partial funding or a referral better serves the person.

5. **Decision** — All benevolence decisions require **unanimous agreement** of the Benevolence Team members involved. The team determines whether to approve, partially approve, or decline, and sets any conditions (e.g., designating funds to a specific bill, recommending a budgeting course). Large or complex cases are escalated to the elders. See [[benevolence-roles|Roles & Confidentiality]] for oversight details.

6. **Disbursement** — Once approved, the church pays the need directly to the service provider or vendor. No cash is given to the recipient except in rare circumstances. See [[benevolence-disbursement|How Aid Is Disbursed]] for the full disbursement policy. All payments are recorded by the Treasurer.

7. **Follow-Up** — Within a few weeks of disbursement, a team member or elder schedules a friendly follow-up to confirm the aid solved the problem, assess ongoing needs, and offer pastoral care, budgeting help, or discipleship support. Financial stewardship counseling may be encouraged or required for significant assistance.
