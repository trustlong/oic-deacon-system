# The 12-Month Rule

Benevolence assistance is designed as **one-time aid**, not ongoing support. A member (or family unit) will normally receive a disbursement only **once within any 12-month period**.

## Why This Rule Exists

The 12-month limit prevents unhealthy dependence, encourages members to use assistance as a springboard to stability, and ensures the fund can serve as many people in crisis as possible. It reflects the biblical principle that the church's aid is a "life jacket," not a sustained subsidy (cf. 2 Thess. 3:10).

## Second Requests Within 12 Months

A second request within 12 months is **normally declined**. It will be referred to church leadership and requires:

- **Extraordinary new circumstances** — a genuinely different, unforeseen crisis (not a continuation of the same underlying situation).
- Elder and Benevolence Team review — the case is treated as exceptional and receives heightened scrutiny.

## Tracking

All disbursements are logged in the confidential benevolence file, including dates and amounts. This record is consulted each time a new request is received, enabling the team to apply the 12-month rule consistently and fairly.

## Long-Term Hardship Exceptions

In rare cases — a severe medical catastrophe, house fire, bereavement, disability, or similar — the church may provide more extended assistance. Even in these exceptions, at least 12 months must generally pass between grants, and substantial justification is required. Long-term cases are handled with elder involvement and are typically paired with discipleship: financial coaching, mentoring, job training, or regular pastoral check-ins.
