# How Aid Is Disbursed

Approved benevolence assistance is **paid directly to the service provider or creditor** on behalf of the recipient — not handed to the member as cash. This protects accountability, ensures funds solve the intended problem, and keeps the church "above reproach."

## Methods by Need Type

| Need | Disbursement Method |
|---|---|
| Utility bill (electric, gas, water) | Check payable to the utility company (with member's account number), or direct online payment to that account. |
| Rent / housing | Check made payable to the landlord or property management company. |
| Medical bill | Payment to the hospital, clinic, or pharmacy. |
| Groceries | Grocery gift card purchased by the church, or a team member accompanies the recipient to buy food. |
| Gas / transportation | Gas card or meeting at a gas station to fill the tank. |
| Other approved expenses | Vendor-direct payment or gift card as appropriate. |

## No Cash to Recipients

**No cash will be given directly to the recipient** except in rare, documented circumstances — and even then, typically only in the form of a restricted gift card. This is not a matter of distrust; it is wise stewardship that protects both the recipient and the church.

## Logistics

- The member provides the bill, invoice, or account information needed for the church to remit payment correctly.
- If timing is critical (e.g., an imminent utility shut-off), the team can often expedite payment by phone or online once approved.
- All disbursements are documented with receipts or confirmation numbers and recorded by the Treasurer as benevolence expenses.
- Non-monetary aid (groceries from the pantry, a donated item) is likewise recorded.

> Benevolence gifts are **true gifts, not loans**. Recipients are never required — or permitted — to repay the fund. Anyone wishing to give out of later gratitude should do so through the general offering.
