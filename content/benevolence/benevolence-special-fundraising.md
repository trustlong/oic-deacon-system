# Special Fundraising

When an approved benevolence need **exceeds the current fund balance**, OIC Church may call for a special designated-giving effort within the congregation rather than decline the request outright.

## How It Works

1. The elders make a **discreet announcement** to the congregation describing the general nature of the need — without naming the individual or sharing identifying details. For example: *"We have an opportunity to assist a brother/sister in a significant crisis above what our benevolence budget can cover this month. If you feel led to contribute, please designate a gift to the Benevolence Fund."*

2. Members may give **earmarked donations** above their regular tithes and offerings, or pledge monthly contributions toward the specific need.

3. All contributions go into the **church Benevolence Fund account** — never directly to the individual — to ensure proper handling and accountability.

4. Once collected, the Benevolence Team disburses the funds to the vendor or provider as usual (see [[benevolence-disbursement|How Aid Is Disbursed]]).

5. Any excess funds collected beyond the specific need remain in the Benevolence Fund for future needs, or are allocated as directed by the elders in line with donors' intent.

## Protecting Dignity

The individual's privacy is always maintained. Announcements describe only the general category of need. Donors trust the elders and deacons to distribute gifts appropriately and do not receive individual reports of who was helped.

## Biblical Basis

This practice follows the New Testament pattern of congregational giving for relief: when believers in Judea faced hardship, other churches took up collections to send aid (2 Cor. 8:13–15; 9:1–12), and the early Jerusalem church pooled resources voluntarily so that no one lacked basics (Acts 4:34–35).
