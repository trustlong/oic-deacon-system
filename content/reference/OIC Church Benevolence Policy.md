# At a Glance: How the Benevolence Fund Works

**Purpose**  
OIC Church’s Benevolence Fund provides short-term, crisis assistance to help meet urgent needs within our church family, in a way that is compassionate, wise, and accountable. (Constitution, Article II–III; Church Covenant)

**Who it’s for**  
**Active members in good standing** who are facing a genuine, temporary financial crisis. (Bylaws, Article 1 – Membership)

**What it covers (priority needs)**  
Basic necessities and emergency needs such as:

* Food and groceries  
* Essential utilities (electric, gas, water)  
* Rent / short-term housing to prevent eviction or homelessness  
* Essential medical care and prescriptions  
* Job-qualifying education or training required for legal employment (with evidence of satisfactory progress, generally GPA 3.0+ or equivalent)

**What it does not cover**

* Non-essential or luxury items/expenses  
* Consumer debt (credit cards, personal loans)  
* Legal fees, fines, bail, or penalties  
* Ongoing routine bills or long-term subsidies  
* Any request that would enable sin or undermine Christian testimony (Church Covenant; Bylaws, Article 1, Section 4\)

**How to apply**  
Contact the Benevolence Deacon/Team or an elder to request the Benevolence Request Form. Submit the form with supporting documentation (e.g., bill/invoice) and a brief explanation of the need.

**How decisions are made**  
Requests are reviewed confidentially by the Benevolence Team, under elder oversight, and approved only with unanimous agreement of the team, with prayer and careful stewardship. (Bylaws, Article 2 – Elders/Deacons)

**How assistance is given**  
Aid is typically provided as direct payment to a vendor/provider (e.g., utility company, landlord, clinic) rather than cash to the member.

**What to expect afterward**  
A brief follow-up to ensure the need was met and to offer additional pastoral care, budgeting help, or discipleship support when appropriate. (Church Covenant)

# OIC Church Benevolence Policy

## Biblical Basis and Purpose

As followers of Christ, we recognize our calling to care for one another’s practical needs as an expression of God’s love. Scripture exhorts us to “do good to everyone, **especially** to those who are of the household of faith” (Galatians 6:10)[\[1\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/contribute-to-the-needs-of-the-saints/%23:~:text=great%252520transforming%252520mercy%252520in%252520Christ,12%25253A1%2525E2%252580%2525932). In fact, the Bible asks how God’s love can abide in someone who “has worldly goods and sees his brother in need” but refuses to help (1 John 3:17)[\[2\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=1%252520John%2525203%25253A17%252520But%252520whoever,of%252520God%252520abide%252520in%252520him). The early church modeled radical generosity – believers even sold possessions so that “**there was not a needy person among them**” (Acts 4:34–35)[\[3\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/contribute-to-the-needs-of-the-saints/%23:~:text=They%252520not%252520only%252520sold%252520their,Acts%2525204%25253A32%2525E2%252580%25259335). Our benevolence ministry (“mercy ministry”) exists to reflect Christ’s mercy by assisting church members in financial hardship, in a way that is compassionate **and** wise[\[4\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=In%252520other%252520words%25252C%252520benevolence%252520is,given%252520dignity). Benevolence is not merely handing out money; it is *showing the mercy and love we ourselves have received in Christ*[\[4\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=In%252520other%252520words%25252C%252520benevolence%252520is,given%252520dignity). Through this ministry we aim to bear one another’s burdens and thus fulfill the law of Christ (Gal. 6:2), while stewarding limited resources responsibly.

## Fund Source and Budget

Under our **Bylaws** (Article 5, Section 1), the church operates under a unified budget plan and designated gifts can only be made to accounts approved by the congregation. The elders are authorized to establish policies and practices for the church consistent with its purposes (Bylaws, Article 2, Section 2b). The Benevolence Fund is therefore established by the elders and used solely for benevolence, separate from the general budget.

OIC Church maintains a designated Benevolence Fund (separate from the general budget) to provide financial aid in times of hardship. This fund is primarily supported through the church’s budgeted allotment and special donations[\[5\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=SOURCE%252520OF%252520FUNDING). For the current year, the church allocates an **annual benevolence budget**, which is reviewed as part of the church’s budgeting process. Benevolence funds are received and held on a rolling basis, and assistance decisions are made in light of the current fund balance and expected ongoing contributions rather than a strict monthly quota. Members may also contribute specifically to this fund as the Lord leads (after faithfully giving tithes/offerings to the general needs of the church[\[6\]](https://eric-turbedsky.squarespace.com/s/benevolence-ministry-2014.pdf%23:~:text=Benevolence%252520Ministry%2525202%252520a,priority%252520is%252520to%252520care%252520for)). The Benevolence Fund is a restricted account used **only** for assisting those in need, not for regular church expenses[\[7\]](https://eric-turbedsky.squarespace.com/s/benevolence-ministry-2014.pdf%23:~:text=Fund%252520unless%252520they%252520are%252520first,priority%252520is%252520to%252520care%252520for). Unused benevolence funds may accumulate for future needs or be reserved for emergency cases, at the discretion of church leadership.

*Importantly, all gifts from the Benevolence Fund are true* *gifts, not loans to be repaid*[\[8\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=Under%252520no%252520circumstance%252520is%252520a,general%252520fund%252520of%252520the%252520church). Recipients are under no obligation to “pay back” assistance, and in fact no repayment will be accepted to the fund (should a recipient wish to give later out of gratitude, they may give to the general offering)[\[8\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=Under%252520no%252520circumstance%252520is%252520a,general%252520fund%252520of%252520the%252520church). This policy keeps our giving one-directional, reflecting God’s grace. The fund is modest, so we rely on the Lord’s provision and the generosity of His people. If benevolence needs exceed our current fund balance (see “Fundraising for Special Needs” below), the church may call for additional voluntary contributions. **All donations for benevolence should go into the church fund, not to individuals directly**, to ensure proper stewardship and accountability in disbursing aid.

## Eligibility – Who Can Apply

Benevolence assistance is intended **for church members only**. Applicants must be active members of OIC Church (covenant members in good standing). For the purposes of this policy, “active members in good standing” refers to those who meet the qualifications for **Active Members** in our Bylaws (Article 1, Section 1)—members who profess faith, are baptized, faithfully attend public worship and ministries, and support the church financially—and who are not dismissed or under church discipline (see Bylaws, Article 1, Sections 3–4). We believe this focus is biblical: our first responsibility is to care for the needs of our spiritual family (Romans 12:13; Galatians 6:10)[\[9\]](https://eric-turbedsky.squarespace.com/s/benevolence-ministry-2014.pdf%23:~:text=4,priority%252520is%252520to%252520care%252520for). By prioritizing members, we follow the New Testament pattern of the church caring for “the saints” – fellow believers – in times of need[\[1\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/contribute-to-the-needs-of-the-saints/%23:~:text=great%252520transforming%252520mercy%252520in%252520Christ,12%25253A1%2525E2%252580%2525932).

Non-members and the broader community are **not** eligible for disbursements from this fund. However, we still desire to show Christ’s love to all. If a stranger or non-member approaches the church for help, we will respond with *limited* mercy aid outside the fund’s scope: for example, offering a grocery gift card, a bag of non-perishable food, or a referral to a local ministry or shelter[\[10\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=,Handing%252520Out%252520Cash)[\[11\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=,of%252520Resources%252520in%252520the%252520Area). We avoid giving cash to walk-in requests, but may keep on hand some grocery vouchers, transit passes, or hygiene kits to provide immediate relief[\[10\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=,Handing%252520Out%252520Cash). We also maintain an updated list of trusted local charities and ministries (food pantries, shelters, counseling services) to which we can refer non-members for further assistance[\[11\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=,of%252520Resources%252520in%252520the%252520Area). In doing good to all, we still “err on the side of mercy” whenever possible[\[12\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=,Side%252520of%252520Mercy), yet our *benevolence fund* proper is reserved for members of the church family. This policy helps us care for our own community responsibly, following the principle that the church should support its *family* and that relatives (and other agencies) should help first in other cases (cf. 1 Tim. 5:8)[\[13\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=congregation%252520might%252520help).

## Priority of Needs Covered

The Benevolence Fund is designed to help with **basic living necessities and emergency needs** during a time of crisis[\[14\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=The%252520stated%252520purpose%252520of%252520the,food%252520from%252520our%252520Blessing%252520Box). Priority is given to needs that are **essential**, urgent, and arise from hardship rather than negligence. Examples of **eligible uses** (typical needs) include:

* **Food and groceries:** e.g. grocery expenses or immediate food supply for a family in need (the church may also provide food directly from a pantry or “blessing box” on campus)[\[15\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=The%252520stated%252520purpose%252520of%252520the,food%252520from%252520our%252520Blessing%252520Box).  
* **Utility bills (essential utilities):** e.g. assistance with an electric or water bill to prevent shut-off (often the *last portion* of a bill may be covered, encouraging the individual to pay what they can)[\[16\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=Needs%252520that%252520can%252520be%252520assisted,with).  
* **Rent or housing shelter:** e.g. a portion of a month’s rent to prevent eviction, or a short-term lodging (hotel) in a crisis[\[17\]](https://www.pastoralcareinc.com/app/download/7126343692/Benevolence+Guidelines.pdf%23:~:text=substances%252520abuse,stay%252520in%252520a%252520motel%25252C%252520etc).  
* **Medical care or prescriptions:** essential medical bills or medication costs that the person cannot cover.  
* **Job‑qualifying education or training:** tuition or certification required for legal employment (especially for those currently lacking work authorisation). Continued assistance in this category requires evidence of satisfactory progress—generally a minimum **GPA of 3.0 or equivalent**—and all training must be lawful.  
* **Other critical expenses:** evaluated case-by-case – for instance, emergency travel for a funeral of an immediate family member, or other compassionate grants as the committee deems appropriate.

These are *illustrative examples*, not an exhaustive list. The guiding principle is to provide **temporary, emergency relief** for necessities, serving as a “life jacket” in a financial storm[\[18\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=%252523%252523%2525205.%252520For%252520Long,Situations%25252C%252520Prioritize%252520Discipleship). Benevolence gifts are intended as a short-term help while the recipient works to resolve the underlying difficulty[\[19\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=Benevolence%252520assistance%252520is%252520intended%252520to,effort%252520to%252520rectify%252520the%252520situation). This fund is a **last resort**, after the person has exhausted personal resources and reasonable alternatives (savings, family support, insurance, etc.)[\[20\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=Benevolence%252520assistance%252520is%252520intended%252520to,effort%252520to%252520rectify%252520the%252520situation). The recipient should also be making a good-faith effort to improve their situation (e.g. seeking employment, budgeting, etc.), rather than depending continuously on church aid[\[20\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=Benevolence%252520assistance%252520is%252520intended%252520to,effort%252520to%252520rectify%252520the%252520situation).

**Ineligible uses:** The church’s benevolence resources are limited, so we cannot cover non-essential or inappropriate expenses. We will generally **NOT** disburse funds for the following purposes[\[21\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=Needs%252520that%252520may%252520NOT%252520be,by%252520the%252520Benevolence%252520Fund%252520include)[\[22\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=):

* *Luxury or non-essential utilities:* e.g. cable TV, internet bills, cell phone upgrades.  
* *Consumer debt or credit card payoffs:* We cannot assume responsibility for paying off personal loans, credit cards, or business investments[\[23\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=)[\[24\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=,payments%252520or%252520irresponsible%252520actions).  
* *Legal fees or fines resulting from criminal behavior:* The fund will not cover bail, court fees, traffic fines, or penalties incurred due to unlawful or irresponsible actions[\[25\]\[26\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=).  
* *Expenses that would enable sin or undermine Christian testimony:* e.g. funding an unmarried couple’s shared housing arrangement[\[27\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=,criminal%252520behavior), subsidizing an addiction or gambling debt[\[22\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=), etc.  
* *Routine or long-term bills beyond emergency relief:* e.g. ongoing childcare costs, elective schooling/tuition, or multiple months’ rent – the fund is not a sustained subsidy program[\[28\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=).

These exclusions reflect our **Church Covenant’s** call to live self‑controlled, upright and godly lives (Titus 2:12) and our **Bylaws’** commitment to lovingly correct unchristian conduct through church discipline (Bylaws, Article 1, Section 4). They help ensure that benevolence does not enable sin or undermine our witness.

Additionally, any applicant who harasses or treats church staff/coordinators disrespectfully will have their request denied[\[29\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=,payments%252520or%252520irresponsible%252520actions). The Benevolence Committee reserves the right to decline requests that don’t align with the fund’s purpose or that might **reinforce negative behavior** (for instance, repeatedly bailing someone out of consequences caused by recklessness or laziness)[\[30\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=It%252520is%252520possible%252520that%252520budget,reinforced%252520by%252520receiving%252520financial%252520assistance). We lovingly uphold the biblical principle that those unwilling to work should not be routinely fed by others (2 Thess. 3:10)[\[31\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=2%252520Thessalonians%2525203%25253A10,and%252520eat%252520their%252520own%252520bread)[\[32\]](https://eric-turbedsky.squarespace.com/s/benevolence-ministry-2014.pdf%23:~:text=4%25252C8%25252C%252520and%25252015%252529,2%252520Thessalonians%2525203%25253A10). In some cases, assistance may be conditional upon the individual pursuing counseling or financial coaching, especially if it appears that a pattern of poor decisions contributed to the need[\[33\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=opportunity%252520for%252520an%252520on,relationship)[\[30\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=It%252520is%252520possible%252520that%252520budget,reinforced%252520by%252520receiving%252520financial%252520assistance). Our goal is to help in ways that truly **heal** and uplift, not enable harmful habits (we aim for mercy **and** wisdom).

## Application Process

Requesting benevolence aid should be as simple and barrier-free as possible, while still giving us the information needed to evaluate the situation fairly. We understand it can be humbling to ask for help, so the process is designed to treat applicants with dignity and care.

1. **Initiating a Request:** A member in need of assistance should contact the Benevolence Team (or designated Deacon of Benevolence) or a pastor/elder to obtain a **Benevolence Request Form**[\[34\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=A%252520Benevolence%252520Request%252520Form%252520,the%252520vendor%25252C%252520never%252520the%252520requestor). This form may be available in the church office or via a private online link. Church leaders will also continually remind the congregation that this fund exists, so that no one suffers in silence out of shame[\[35\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=Churches%252520should%252520continually%252520remind%252520members,must%252520communicate%252520their%252520needs%25252C%252520too). We encourage a culture of openness where asking for help is met with understanding, not judgment[\[36\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=must%252520communicate%252520their%252520needs%25252C%252520too).  
2. **Completing the Form:** The application form is brief and confidential. It asks basic questions to clarify the need and guide our response. For example, the form will likely include:  
3. **Member Information:** Name, contact details, and confirmation of church membership (only members may apply).  
4. **Description of Need:** A short explanation of what assistance is needed and *why*. (e.g. “Behind on electric bill due to job loss,” or “Unexpected medical expense”).  
5. **Amount Requested:** The dollar amount or specific bill you need help with (attach a copy of the bill or invoice, if applicable)[\[34\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=A%252520Benevolence%252520Request%252520Form%252520,the%252520vendor%25252C%252520never%252520the%252520requestor).  
6. **Reason and Circumstances:** What led to this need? (This helps distinguish between a one-time crisis versus ongoing financial struggles, etc.)  
7. **Other Resources Explored:** Have you sought any help elsewhere or taken steps on your own? (e.g. talked to family per 1 Tim 5:4, applied for assistance programs, used savings)[\[20\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=Benevolence%252520assistance%252520is%252520intended%252520to,effort%252520to%252520rectify%252520the%252520situation).  
8. **Household Budget Info (if applicable):** For larger requests, the form might ask for a brief summary of income/expenses or why shortfall exists, to help us counsel wisely. (We do *not* require detailed financial statements for a small one-time need, but we may discuss budgeting in person as needed.)  
9. **Consent and Verification:** A signature giving the church permission to verify the information and follow up on the situation[\[37\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=Those%252520requesting%252520assistance%252520must%252520be,any%252520of%252520the%252520information%252520provided). We may ask for a **photo ID** or proof of identity when the request is submitted, to prevent fraud and ensure we correctly identify the individual[\[38\]](https://www.pastoralcareinc.com/app/download/7126343692/Benevolence+Guidelines.pdf%23:~:text=The%252520person%252520must%252520write%252520down,time%252520gift%25252C%252520monthly%25252C%252520first%252520come%25252Ffirst).

*Our benevolence form is kept intentionally straightforward*[\[39\]](https://www.pastoralcareinc.com/app/download/7126343692/Benevolence+Guidelines.pdf%23:~:text=strongly%252520suggested%252520that%252520everyone%252520making,be%252520presented%252520to%252520reduce%252520fraud). It should take only a few minutes to fill out. If an applicant needs help completing it (due to stress or literacy issues), a team member can assist in person. All personal information will remain **confidential** among those directly involved in the review (see Confidentiality section below). The form and any supporting documents (bills, receipts) are filed securely so that repeat requests can be tracked and misuse prevented[\[40\]](https://www.pastoralcareinc.com/app/download/7126343692/Benevolence+Guidelines.pdf%23:~:text=first%252520come%25252Ffirst%252520served%25252C%252520etc,hopes%252520of%252520gaining%252520more%252520benefits).

1. **Submission:** The completed request form (with any required attachments, like a copy of the bill needing payment)[\[34\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=A%252520Benevolence%252520Request%252520Form%252520,the%252520vendor%25252C%252520never%252520the%252520requestor) should be submitted to the Benevolence Team or church office. In urgent situations, a verbal request can be made and the form filled out as soon as possible thereafter. We strive to respond promptly to urgent needs (often within a few days or sooner for emergencies).

## Review and Approval Process

Once a benevolence application is received, the **Benevolence Team** will review it promptly. In accordance with our **Bylaws** (Article 2, Sections 2b–3b), the elders oversee the ministry, resources, and business of the church, and the deacons care for the temporal needs of members. The Benevolence Team therefore operates under elder oversight and includes deacons appointed to oversee member care. OIC’s Benevolence Team will likely consist of one or more deacons appointed to oversee member care, along with an elder overseeing them[\[41\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=,or%252520a%252520Deacon%252520Team)[\[42\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=In%252520order%252520to%252520care%252520for,regularly%252520shared%252520with%252520the%252520elders). (In line with Acts 6:1–7, we entrust this ministry to faithful individuals “full of the Spirit and wisdom”[\[43\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=minister%252520and%252520discern%252520the%252520best,policies%252520and%252520procedures%252520giving%252520oversight), so that needs are handled with both compassion and integrity.) The review process generally includes the following steps:

* **Initial Screening:** The team verifies that the applicant is a member and that the request falls within our scope (see *Eligibility* and *Priority of Needs* above). If the request is clearly outside the policy (for example, asking for something not covered), the team will likely deny it upfront with an explanation, rather than raising false hopes[\[21\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=Needs%252520that%252520may%252520NOT%252520be,by%252520the%252520Benevolence%252520Fund%252520include)[\[22\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=).  
* **Information Gathering:** A designated deacon or team member may reach out to the applicant for a personal conversation (in person or by phone). This *pastoral consultation* is done to clarify details, express care, and determine the best way to help[\[44\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=Every%252520benevolence%252520request%252520must%252520be,or%252520family%252520asking%252520for%252520assistance). We may ask gentle questions about the situation, such as how the need arose, what efforts have been made to address it, and whether there are underlying issues we should be aware of[\[13\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=congregation%252520might%252520help). This conversation is *not* meant to pry unnecessarily, but to ensure we truly help in the wisest way (sometimes a financial gift alone may not solve the core problem). For example, if someone needs help with dental work, and we discover a dentist in the church is willing to assist, that could be a solution *in addition* to or instead of monetary aid[\[45\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=The%252520application%252520forces%252520the%252520individual,in%252520the%252520congregation%252520might%252520help). Or if the individual has significant budgeting challenges, we might arrange financial counseling alongside any gift[\[33\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=opportunity%252520for%252520an%252520on,relationship). All discussions are done in a spirit of love, not interrogation, respecting the member’s dignity.  
* **Evaluation Criteria:** Each request is evaluated on its own merits[\[46\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=,on%252520Its%252520Own%252520Merits). The team prayerfully considers factors like: the **severity and urgency** of the need, the **essential nature** of the expense, the member’s **personal responsibility** in the situation, and the **available funds**. We also consider biblical principles – for instance, we encourage family members to support each other first if possible (1 Tim. 5:8)[\[13\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=congregation%252520might%252520help), and we take into account whether the person is striving to be financially responsible (2 Thess. 3:10 warns against enabling idleness)[\[31\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=2%252520Thessalonians%2525203%25253A10,and%252520eat%252520their%252520own%252520bread). However, we aim to err on the side of grace; a person will not be disqualified for making an honest mistake or simply because their need resulted from bad luck or difficult providence. *“Be wise and discerning, but also remember that if you have never been taken advantage of, perhaps you’re not being as merciful as you ought to be,”* as one guide cautions[\[12\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=,Side%252520of%252520Mercy). Thus, we balance prudence with generosity.  
* **Decision Making:** After reviewing the request and relevant information, the Benevolence Team decides on an appropriate response. All benevolence decisions require unanimous agreement among the Benevolence Team members involved. The team will determine **whether to approve assistance, and if so, the amount and any conditions** (e.g. requiring the funds go to a specific bill, or recommending the individual attend a budgeting class for ongoing situations)[\[33\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=opportunity%252520for%252520an%252520on,relationship). Because the Benevolence Fund is limited, the team may not be able to grant the full amount requested if it would significantly deplete the current fund balance or hinder the church’s ability to respond to future needs. We may choose to **partially fund** a request or prioritize urgent essentials first. For example, if multiple requests come in around the same time, we might allocate smaller grants to each so that everyone receives some help, rather than fully funding one and denying others. The team seeks wisdom from God in these allocations. When a request would materially reduce the fund’s ability to meet anticipated needs in the coming months, the team may seek counsel from the elders [\[47\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=Some%252520churches%252520have%252520benevolence%252520funds,they%252520consult%252520with%252520the%252520elders)[\[48\]](https://eric-turbedsky.squarespace.com/s/benevolence-ministry-2014.pdf%23:~:text=9,a%252520member%252520of%252520the%252520committee). However, urgent aid will not be delayed solely for procedural reasons.  
* **Approval and Notification:** Once a decision is reached, the applicant will be informed promptly. If approved, the team communicates the amount and the form of assistance (and may coordinate with the member on how the payment will be made to the vendor – see *Disbursement Process* below). If the request is *denied or deferred*, the team will kindly explain the reason, and whenever possible, provide counsel or referrals for alternative help. Even when we must say “no” due to limits or policy, we strive to show care for the person’s situation, perhaps praying with them or finding non-financial ways to support them. All decisions are documented in the confidential benevolence file, to track how funds are used and to note if the person has received help before (which can affect future requests)[\[40\]](https://www.pastoralcareinc.com/app/download/7126343692/Benevolence+Guidelines.pdf%23:~:text=first%252520come%25252Ffirst%252520served%25252C%252520etc,hopes%252520of%252520gaining%252520more%252520benefits).  
* **Confidentiality:** Throughout the review process, confidentiality is maintained. Maintaining confidentiality also honors our Church Covenant’s commitment to walk together in love, put away injurious speech, and eagerly maintain the unity of the Spirit. Those involved in evaluating and approving requests are reminded to handle personal information with utmost privacy[\[49\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=CONFIDENTIALITY). Details of a member’s financial troubles are never gossip for the church; they remain within the benevolence team and pastoral leadership. (If broader counsel is needed, the team may share necessary info with an elder or trusted advisor, but **always in confidence**.) This protects the dignity of applicants. Even the fact that someone received assistance is not publicized without permission. The congregation may be generally informed that the benevolence fund is being used to bless people, but individual names and circumstances stay private.

## Guidelines for Assistance Decisions

To ensure fairness and wise stewardship, OIC Church will use the following guidelines when deciding **how much assistance to provide** and how often:

* **One-Time Assistance (Primary Model):** Benevolence gifts are generally intended as **one-time aid** rather than ongoing support[\[50\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=Gifts%252520from%252520the%252520benevolence%252520fund,should%252520expect%252520to%252520be%252520declined). In most cases, a member (or family unit) will receive a benevolence disbursement only once within a 12-month period. Repeated or long-term financial support from the church is *not* the norm, so that dependence is not created. After receiving help once, further requests within the next year will be referred to church leadership and likely declined[\[50\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=Gifts%252520from%252520the%252520benevolence%252520fund,should%252520expect%252520to%252520be%252520declined), unless there are truly exceptional new circumstances. This policy encourages members to use the gift as a springboard to stability and seek other long-term solutions.  
* **Exceptions for Extreme Needs:** In unusual cases (particularly for active, long-time members facing a severe crisis), the church may decide to assist more than once or for an extendedperiod[\[51\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=In%252520unusual%252520circumstances%25252C%252520particularly%252520involving,approved%252520without%252520new%252520extenuating%252520circumstances). Such cases are handled with discretion by the elders and benevolence team. Generally, even in these cases, at least 12 months should pass before a second request is considered, and substantial justification (extenuating circumstances beyond the person’s control) is required[\[51\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=In%252520unusual%252520circumstances%25252C%252520particularly%252520involving,approved%252520without%252520new%252520extenuating%252520circumstances). The heart behind this exception is to care for our church family in truly dire situations (e.g. a house fire, a medical catastrophe, a bereaved family with ongoing needs), while still encouraging long-term solutions and involvement of the whole congregation if needed.  
  * **Guidelines on Amount per Case:** Because the Benevolence Fund is limited, the team may set a guideline that no single individual’s request should consume an undue portion of the **current** fund balance, so that multiple needs can be met. Each situation will be considered case-by-case, and the exact amount approved will depend on the urgency of the need, the available fund balance, and what is **fair and helpful**. Larger amounts may be granted if special fundraising is done or designated gifts are provided. The church’s philosophy is to give a meaningful helping hand *within our means* – trusting God to multiply our “five loaves and two fish” to meet needs as we act in faith.  
* **Personal Responsibility and Follow-Through:** We require that the recipient be actively working, when able, to improve their circumstances. If someone is *able-bodied but unwilling to work or seek work*, that person would **not** normally qualify for financial assistance[\[32\]](https://eric-turbedsky.squarespace.com/s/benevolence-ministry-2014.pdf%23:~:text=4%25252C8%25252C%252520and%25252015%252529,2%252520Thessalonians%2525203%25253A10), in keeping with biblical teaching (2 Thess. 3:10)[\[31\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=2%252520Thessalonians%2525203%25253A10,and%252520eat%252520their%252520own%252520bread). Likewise, if someone persists in irresponsible spending or habits that continually cause financial crises, the church may insist on counseling or other changes as a condition for any help[\[30\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=It%252520is%252520possible%252520that%252520budget,reinforced%252520by%252520receiving%252520financial%252520assistance). Those struggling with underlying issues (addiction, etc.) may be directed to appropriate counseling or recovery programs alongside any financial aid. Our aim is **redemptive** help – addressing root problems so the cycle of need can be broken, to the glory of God. When long-term benevolence is given (e.g. repeated help to a widow, a disabled member, or others with ongoing challenges), the church will prioritize *discipleship* in tandem with aid – this might include financial literacy classes, mentoring, job training, or regular pastoral check-ins[\[33\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=opportunity%252520for%252520an%252520on,relationship). In all cases, the recipient should demonstrate a good-faith effort to cooperate with any plan or counsel provided, as we partner together toward stability[\[33\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=opportunity%252520for%252520an%252520on,relationship).  
* **Notifying if Situation Changes:** We expect applicants to be honest about their needs. If a member’s situation improves or they receive funds from elsewhere after applying (for instance, another source covers the bill), we ask that they inform the Benevolence Team, so that church funds can be redirected to others or adjusted accordingly. Transparency helps us use God’s resources wisely for those truly in need.

These guidelines are not to be cold rules, but to promote **fairness, accountability, and long-term good** for each person we help. The church’s approach is to provide enough help to truly make a difference, while not fostering unhealthy dependence. As a safeguard, **all benevolence decisions and disbursements are subject to oversight by the elders**, and financial records of the fund are reviewed for integrity[\[42\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=In%252520order%252520to%252520care%252520for,regularly%252520shared%252520with%252520the%252520elders). The deacon(s) administering the fund have authority to act within this policy, but they also report regularly to the elders for accountability and guidance[\[42\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=In%252520order%252520to%252520care%252520for,regularly%252520shared%252520with%252520the%252520elders).

## Fundraising for Special Needs

Occasionally, a member’s financial need may **exceed** the available resources in the Benevolence Fund (for example, a member faces a $1,000 emergency but the fund only has $500 on hand). In such cases, OIC Church will consider a special *fundraising effort* within the congregation to meet the need, following biblical patterns of generosity. In the New Testament, when believers in Judea were suffering, other churches took up collections to send relief (2 Cor. 8:13-15, 9:1-12). Likewise, the early Jerusalem church pooled resources voluntarily so that no one lacked basics[\[3\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/contribute-to-the-needs-of-the-saints/%23:~:text=They%252520not%252520only%252520sold%252520their,Acts%2525204%25253A32%2525E2%252580%25259335). This *voluntary, congregational giving* is a biblical way to address large needs as the family of God.

If a benevolence request is approved but goes beyond the fund’s current capacity, the elders may make a discreet announcement to the members, describing the need (while **maintaining the individual’s privacy** as much as possible). For example, the church might announce: *“We have an opportunity to assist a brother/sister in a significant crisis above what our benevolence budget can cover this month. If you feel led to contribute, please designate a gift to the Benevolence Fund.”* Members can then give earmarked donations above their regular giving. We may also invite **pledges** (e.g. a few members might pledge $100 each month toward the need). All such contributions will be directed into the **Benevolence Fund account**, **not given directly to the individual**, to ensure proper handling and accountability[\[52\]](https://eric-turbedsky.squarespace.com/s/benevolence-ministry-2014.pdf%23:~:text=Benevolence%252520Ministry%2525202%252520a,James%2525201%25253A27). Once collected, the funds will be disbursed by the church to fulfill the need as planned. (If by God’s grace excess money comes in beyond the specific need, it will remain in the Benevolence Fund for future needs, or be allocated as directed by the elders in line with the donors’ intent.)

Finally, the **leadership will ensure** that any public fundraising is done in a way that preserves the **dignity** of the person in need. We do not announce names or details without consent. In many cases, only the general need will be mentioned. Donors are asked to trust the elders/deacons to distribute their gifts appropriately, as we maintain confidentiality. We believe that as members “have compassion on their fellow believers,” God will provide through His church family[\[3\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/contribute-to-the-needs-of-the-saints/%23:~:text=They%252520not%252520only%252520sold%252520their,Acts%2525204%25253A32%2525E2%252580%25259335)[\[53\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/contribute-to-the-needs-of-the-saints/%23:~:text=These%252520early%252520church%252520members%252520had,became%252520a%252520powerful%252520evangelistic%252520witness).

## Disbursement (Cash-Out) Process

When a benevolence request is approved, OIC Church will typically **pay the aid directly to the service provider or creditor** on behalf of the recipient, rather than giving cash to the individual. This is both for accountability and to help ensure the funds solve the intended problem[\[54\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=,Merchants%252520Directly). For example, if the assistance is for an electric bill, the church will issue a check **payable to the utility company** (with the member’s account number) or pay it online to that account[\[34\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=A%252520Benevolence%252520Request%252520Form%252520,the%252520vendor%25252C%252520never%252520the%252520requestor). If the need is groceries, the church may purchase a grocery gift card or accompany the person to buy food, rather than just handing out money[\[10\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=,Handing%252520Out%252520Cash). For gas, a gas card or meeting at a gas station to fill the tank could be arranged[\[17\]](https://www.pastoralcareinc.com/app/download/7126343692/Benevolence+Guidelines.pdf%23:~:text=substances%252520abuse,stay%252520in%252520a%252520motel%25252C%252520etc). In cases of rent, a check can be made out to the landlord or property management. Medical bills can be paid to the hospital or clinic. **No cash will be given directly** to the recipient except in rare circumstances (and even then, usually in the form of a restricted gift card). This practice guards against misuse of funds and maintains clear records[\[54\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=,Merchants%252520Directly). It is not a matter of distrust, but of being “above reproach” and exercising wise stewardship in God’s sight[\[54\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=,Merchants%252520Directly).

The Benevolence Team will coordinate the payment logistics with the recipient. The member may need to provide a bill, invoice, or account info so that the church can remit payment correctly[\[34\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=A%252520Benevolence%252520Request%252520Form%252520,the%252520vendor%25252C%252520never%252520the%252520requestor). We ask for prompt cooperation in supplying any needed documents (for example, a copy of the lease or utility bill). If timing is critical (e.g. an imminent cutoff notice), the team can often expedite a payment by phone or online once approved. All disbursements will be documented with receipts or confirmation numbers, and recorded by the church finance treasurer as benevolence expenses. The deacon or treasurer will keep a file of what was paid, to whom, and on what date, along with the application form, for accountability purposes.

If a benevolence gift is *non-monetary* (such as groceries from the church pantry, or a donated item like a medical device), the team will note the assistance given in kind. In some cases, the church might utilize a third-party ministry: for example, if we partner with a local food bank or counseling center, we might cover the **cost** of that service for the member instead of giving funds to the member. Those arrangements will likewise be handled with proper communication and records.

In summary, **the church pays for needs directly whenever feasible**[\[54\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=,Merchants%252520Directly). We avoid handing out cash to prevent both temptation and any appearance of impropriety. This policy protects the recipient as well – for instance, a person struggling with addiction is better served if we pay their rent directly rather than give cash that might pose a temptation. It also protects the church, by ensuring donations are used for their intended purpose (fuel for mercy, not funding unrelated purchases). We want to “leave no room for the flesh” in these matters[\[54\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=,Merchants%252520Directly), aiming for the highest ethical and financial integrity.

## Follow-Up and Accountability

Our care for members in need doesn’t end once a check is written. **Follow-up** is a crucial part of the benevolence process, ensuring that the assistance given has been effective and that the individual is supported going forward. After a disbursement, a member of the Benevolence Team or an elder will schedule a follow-up conversation with the recipient within a few weeks. This conversation is friendly and caring – we want to know how you’re doing, whether the crisis has abated, and how we can continue to pray or help (perhaps in non-financial ways). If the person’s situation is still difficult, we will discuss any next steps, which could include connecting them with longer-term help (job opportunities, mentoring, etc.) or, if appropriate, evaluating a subsequent request in the future (per the one-time policy exceptions).

This follow‑up care reflects our Church Covenant promise to carry each other’s burdens and rejoice and weep together (Gal. 6:2; Rom. 12:15), and it fulfills the **Bylaws**’ call for members to encourage one another toward holiness and love (Article 1, Section 2).

For those who received significant assistance, the church may encourage or require participation in **financial stewardship counseling or training**. This is not punitive, but aimed at empowerment. For example, we might invite the person to meet with a Christian financial counselor to work on a budget, or to attend a workshop on money management (like a “Financial Peace” course) – especially if the trial revealed underlying financial illiteracy or debt issues[\[33\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=opportunity%252520for%252520an%252520on,relationship). James 1:27 reminds us that true religion cares for those in distress (like widows and orphans)[\[55\]](https://eric-turbedsky.squarespace.com/s/benevolence-ministry-2014.pdf%23:~:text=3,priority%252520is%252520to%252520care%252520for), and part of caring is equipping people with tools to improve their situation. Similarly, if emotional or spiritual issues are involved (such as depression from job loss, or marital strain due to finances), pastoral follow-up and possibly a referral to counseling or a support group will be offered. **Our goal is holistic restoration**, not just a one-time handout. We want to walk alongside members through the trial, integrating practical aid with discipleship and prayer.

The Benevolence Team will also follow up to verify that the funds solved the need intended. For instance, we might confirm that the utility payment posted to the member’s account correctly, or that they were able to use the gift card without issue. Any irregularities or issues will be addressed. We also appreciate feedback from recipients – hearing how the assistance helped can encourage the team (and, with permission, testimonies may be shared anonymously with the congregation to encourage generosity).

**Accountability:** The benevolence ministry operates under oversight to remain above reproach. The deacon(s) in charge report to the elders and ultimately to the congregation (in broad terms). All transactions are logged by the finance team. Periodically, a summary report of the Benevolence Fund can be reviewed by the church’s finance committee or elder board – for example, noting how much was disbursed in a quarter and for how many cases (without breaching individual confidentiality). This ensures transparency that funds are used for ministry purposes. The deacons have discretion day-to-day, but they **check in regularly with the elders** about ongoing benevolence cases and expenditures[\[42\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=In%252520order%252520to%252520care%252520for,regularly%252520shared%252520with%252520the%252520elders). If a case is complex or sensitive, the elders will provide guidance and pastoral input. The church’s normal financial controls (dual signatures on checks above certain amounts, receipts kept, etc.) apply to benevolence disbursements as well. By having multiple layers of oversight, we **protect the integrity** of the fund and those administering it[\[42\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=In%252520order%252520to%252520care%252520for,regularly%252520shared%252520with%252520the%252520elders).

Finally, we recognize that mercy ministry requires wisdom. We pray for discernment to truly help without unintentionally harming. If mistakes are made or if someone does take advantage of the church’s kindness, we will learn and adjust the policy as needed – but we would rather **be overly generous than overly strict**, if we must err[\[12\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=,Side%252520of%252520Mercy). We take to heart the counsel to be careful *and* caring in mercy ministry: *“We need to be careful, but we also need to care.”*[\[56\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/journalwe-need-be-careful-we-also-need-care/%23:~:text=We%252520Need%252520to%252520Be%252520Careful%25252C,from%252520less%252520immediately%252520rewarding)[\[12\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=,Side%252520of%252520Mercy) In all things, we depend on God’s grace to supply the needs of His people.

**Confidentiality Note:** All records of benevolence applications and assistance are kept confidential. Only the Benevolence Team and relevant church leaders will know the identities and details. Donors from the congregation who contribute to the fund do not get to direct funds to specific individuals nor receive detailed reports of who was helped – they trust the church leadership to distribute aid justly. This protects everyone’s privacy. We may share anonymized stories or general updates to show how the fund is helping (“for example: this month the benevolence fund helped a family with rent after a job loss[\[53\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/contribute-to-the-needs-of-the-saints/%23:~:text=These%252520early%252520church%252520members%252520had,became%252520a%252520powerful%252520evangelistic%252520witness)”), but without names or identifying specifics.

## Conclusion

In summary, the OIC Church Benevolence Policy outlines a compassionate yet structured approach to financial assistance for members. We limit aid to church members to fulfill the biblical mandate of caring “for the needs of the saints” first[\[9\]](https://eric-turbedsky.squarespace.com/s/benevolence-ministry-2014.pdf%23:~:text=4,priority%252520is%252520to%252520care%252520for), while not neglecting to show Christ’s love to others through smaller acts of mercy. We prioritize urgent, basic needs and strive to meet them in a fair manner within our means. The application and review process is designed to be simple, dignifying, and thorough, involving appropriate oversight by trusted leaders. We handle disbursements with accountability and prefer direct payments to ensure integrity[\[34\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=A%252520Benevolence%252520Request%252520Form%252520,the%252520vendor%25252C%252520never%252520the%252520requestor)[\[54\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=,Merchants%252520Directly). We also commit to follow up with recipients, offering counsel and community support so that financial aid becomes a step toward growth and not a crutch[\[33\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=opportunity%252520for%252520an%252520on,relationship). This policy is established under the authority of the One In Christ Church Constitution, Bylaws and Church Covenant. In the event of any conflict, those governing documents control, and the elders and congregation may amend this policy consistent with them. All of this is done in reliance on God’s provision and guided by biblical principles of generosity, stewardship, and care for one another[\[1\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/contribute-to-the-needs-of-the-saints/%23:~:text=great%252520transforming%252520mercy%252520in%252520Christ,12%25253A1%2525E2%252580%2525932)[\[2\]](https://www.fbcprincetonwv.com/benevolence-policy%23:~:text=1%252520John%2525203%25253A17%252520But%252520whoever,of%252520God%252520abide%252520in%252520him).

May this policy help OIC Church to be a shining example of Christ’s mercy – a family where, by God’s grace, *“there is not a needy person among us”* (Acts 4:34)[\[3\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/contribute-to-the-needs-of-the-saints/%23:~:text=They%252520not%252520only%252520sold%252520their,Acts%2525204%25253A32%2525E2%252580%25259335) because we lovingly bear each other’s burdens. As we administer the Benevolence Fund, we remember that ultimately **Jesus is the one who meets all our needs**[\[57\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/principles-for-a-benevolence-policy-that-is-both-merciful-and-wise/%23:~:text=I%252520love%252520Peter%2525E2%252580%252599s%252520example%252520in,can%252520always%252520give%252520people%252520Jesus). Our prayer is that every act of aid points back to Him, glorifying God as we “contribute to the needs of the saints” (Rom. 12:13) and demonstrate the reality of the gospel through practical love[\[58\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/contribute-to-the-needs-of-the-saints/%23:~:text=God%2525E2%252580%252599s%252520Word%252520tells%252520us%252520we,12%25253A13)[\[1\]](https://www.9marks.org/article/contribute-to-the-needs-of-the-saints/%23:~:text=great%252520transforming%252520mercy%252520in%252520Christ,12%25253A1%2525E2%252580%2525932).

# OIC Benevolence Fund — Member Version

## Summary

This summary explains how members of OIC Church can request financial help during a crisis. It condenses the full Benevolence Policy for quick reference and highlights the main principles.

## Eligibility — Who Can Apply

• Active members only: The Benevolence Fund serves the OIC church family in good standing.

• Last resort: Applicants should first exhaust savings, family support, insurance or other resources.

• Commitment to improvement: Members should be working toward stability (seeking employment, budgeting, discipleship) and willing to engage in counseling if recommended.

## What the Fund Helps With

• Food and groceries when you cannot afford to feed yourself or your family.

• Essential utilities (electricity, gas, water) to avoid shut-off.

• Rent or short-term lodging to prevent eviction or homelessness.

• Medical care or prescriptions when insurance and savings are insufficient.

• Job-qualifying education or training when required for legal employment, provided the member maintains satisfactory progress (e.g., 3.0 GPA or equivalent).

• Other critical needs assessed on a case-by-case basis, such as emergency travel for a family funeral.

## How to Apply

• Request help confidentially by contacting the Benevolence Team, a designated deacon of benevolence, or an elder.

• Complete a short Benevolence Request Form describing the need, amount requested, circumstances, and other resources explored.

• Provide documentation (e.g., copy of the bill or invoice) and, when asked, a brief household budget or proof of membership.

• Consent to verification and follow-up conversations with the benevolence team, who may suggest counseling or financial coaching as part of the process.

## What to Expect

• Compassionate and confidential review by the Benevolence Team, overseen by elders to ensure fairness and wisdom.

• Assistance is generally one-time and modest; repeated requests within a 12month period are unusual and require extraordinary circumstances.

• Approved aid is paid directly to service providers or vendors rather than given as cash.

• Followup and accountability: the church will check in to ensure the need was met and may recommend further support or financial coaching.

## Key Principles

• The Benevolence Fund reflects Christ’s mercy and calls us to bear one another’s burdens while stewarding limited resources responsibly.

• Assistance is a gift, not a loan; recipients are never required or permitted to pay it back.

• Personal responsibility matters: the fund does not cover luxury items, consumer debt, legal fines, or ongoing expenses unrelated to crisis relief.

• The church encourages members to give generously to the Benevolence Fund after their regular tithes and offerings, so that "there is not a needy person among us" (Acts 4:34).
