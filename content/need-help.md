> *"They chose seven men… to serve tables — so that no one would be overlooked."*
> — Acts 6:1–6

Our deacons exist for one purpose: to make sure no one in our church family carries a practical need alone. If something is weighing on you — a bill you can't pay, a ride you need, a meal after you come home from the hospital, a mountain of paperwork you don't understand — **please ask us.** That is exactly what we are here for.

**You do not need to know who to ask or how the church works.** Just tell us the need. We will figure out the rest.

---

## What kinds of needs?

Here are some examples — organized by situation — so you can see yourself in them. If yours isn't listed, ask anyway.

### New to this country / immigrants
- Guidance on getting a driver's license, Social Security card, or state ID
- Setting up a bank account, phone plan, or utilities
- Finding an ESL class or language help
- Understanding government paperwork or forms *(we connect you with the right resource; we are not legal advisors)*
- Finding affordable groceries or household goods

See also: [[self-help/self-help-immigrants|Self-Help Resources for Immigrants]]

### International students
- Getting from the airport and settling in when you first arrive
- Finding furniture, household items, or a place to store belongings over summer
- Transportation around town and to church
- Connecting with a host family for a holiday or school break
- General questions about life in Lynchburg

See also: [[self-help/self-help-international-students|Self-Help for International Students]]

### College students
- A ride when you're stuck
- Help with a move at the start or end of the year
- Knowing what resources exist when money gets tight
- Feeling connected when you're far from home

See also: [[self-help/self-help-college-students|Self-Help for College Students]]

### Families
- A meal when a baby arrives, illness hits, or there is a loss
- Help coordinating care around a surgery or recovery
- Connecting to childcare or parenting resources
- Financial hardship — groceries, a past-due bill, rent

See also: [[self-help/self-help-families|Self-Help for Families]]

### Seniors
- A ride to a medical appointment, church, or errands
- Navigating Medicare or benefits paperwork
- Help around the house — a repair, a project, heavy lifting
- Technology questions — phone, tablet, a Zoom call

See also: [[self-help/self-help-seniors|Self-Help for Seniors]]

### Financial hardship
- An urgent bill — utilities, rent, medical, groceries
- Connecting to community assistance programs
- Benevolence — our church's own last-resort fund

If financial help is what you need, see [[benevolence-who-qualifies|who qualifies for benevolence]] and [[benevolence-eligible-needs|what it covers]]. Also see: [[self-help/self-help-financial|Financial Self-Help Resources]]

## How to ask for help

The simplest way:

### ➡️ [Fill out our request form](https://script.google.com/macros/s/AKfycbymhYETeXtj8m8Lnfi3GDx4Zj90PC4kU3gLq0R2JstgTjIke9p0H1Ogd0e2VJlmyRCq5Q/exec)

It takes a couple of minutes and goes straight to the deacon on duty, who will reach out to you. You can fill it out for yourself, or for someone else in the church.

You can also reach us:

- **On a Sunday** — speak to any deacon in person. (Not sure who the deacons are? Ask any elder, shepherd, or cell group leader — they will point you to one.)
- **Through someone you trust** — an elder, a shepherd, a cell group leader, or anyone else who knows your situation can fill out the form or pass the request along on your behalf. You do not have to ask for yourself.

### What to share

To help us respond quickly, tell us what you can:

1. **Who** the need is for (yourself, or someone else)
2. **What** you need
3. **How urgent** it is — is something at risk today or this week, or is there more time?
4. **The best way and time** to reach you
5. Anything you have **already tried**

You will never be judged for asking. What you share stays confidential within the small circle of deacons and leaders working to help you.

## Try self-help first

For many common needs, we maintain a library of free resources — local and national — that you can use right now without waiting. Check [[self-help/index|Self-Help Resources]] first. If the resources there aren't enough, or you'd rather have a person help you navigate, come straight to us.

## A note on spiritual needs

Deacons serve **tangible, practical** needs. For **spiritual** care — counseling, prayer, questions of faith, marriage or family struggles — those are entrusted to our **elders** (pastors and shepherds). If you bring a spiritual need to a deacon, they will warmly connect you to an elder rather than trying to handle it themselves. See [[handbook-tangible-vs-spiritual|how we direct different kinds of needs]].
