Our deacons exist to serve the **tangible needs** of our church family — following the example of **Acts 6**, where servants were appointed so that no one in the congregation would be overlooked. If you have a practical need, we want to help you carry it.

> **You do not need to figure out *who* to ask.** Tell us the need, and the deacons will route it to the right person.

## What kinds of needs?

Bring us anything practical, for example:

- **Financial / material** — help with an urgent bill, groceries, rent, medical costs ([[benevolence/index|Benevolence]])
- **Around the building or home** — a repair, setup help, moving, a project
- **Getting somewhere** — a ride to church, an appointment, the airport
- **Care logistics** — a meal after a birth, surgery, or loss; help during illness
- **Connecting** — you need to reach a ministry, leader, or resource and don't know how
- **Anything else** — if you're not sure, ask anyway. We'll help you find the right path.

## How to make a request

Until our online request form is live, reach out any of these ways:

- Speak to **any deacon** in person on a Sunday
- Email the deacons *(address to be added)*
- Ask your **cell group leader**, an **elder**, or a **shepherd** to pass it along on your behalf

Requests can come **directly from you**, or be **relayed by an elder, shepherd, cell group leader, or anyone** who knows of your need.

### What to include

To help us respond quickly, share what you can:

1. **Who** the need is for (you, or someone you're helping)
2. **What** you need
3. **How urgent** it is — is something at risk *today/this week*, or is there more time?
4. **Best way and time** to reach you
5. Anything you've **already tried**

You will **never** be judged for asking. What you share is kept **confidential** within the small group of deacons and leaders who help.

## What happens next

1. A deacon **acknowledges** your request and becomes your point of contact.
2. They route it to the right **function** (Administration, Operations, Communications, or Finance) — or directly to a church member who has the right resource or skill to help.
3. They **follow up** with you so the need is actually met — not lost.

*Specific response timelines for urgent vs. non-urgent needs are being finalized and will be posted here.*

## A note on spiritual needs

Deacons care for **tangible** needs. For **spiritual** care — counseling, prayer, questions of faith, marriage or family struggles — those are entrusted to our **elders** (our pastors and shepherds). If you bring a spiritual need to a deacon, they will gently connect you to an elder. See [[handbook-tangible-vs-spiritual|how needs are directed]].
